Onboarding Experience

Transforming New Hire Experience

Replacing fragmented onboarding with a structured 30-60-90 day learning journey.

Single source of truth Faster time-to-productivity
+

Challenge

Onboarding information — processes, tools, system access, resources, and points of contact — was scattered across multiple sources and heavily reliant on a buddy. This created inconsistent experiences and delayed new hires' ability to contribute.

Strategy

  • Reduce information overload by prioritising relevant resources
  • Create a single, organised experience instead of scattered sources
  • Provide guidance at the right time through a phased journey

How I Think About Learning

I start with the business problem — not the content.

"The success measure isn't, 'Did they complete the course?' It's whether they can look at a business situation, make a sound decision, and explain why."

Too often, learning starts with "what content do we need to teach?" I start with a different question: what should people do differently — and what experience will help them get there?

That means working backwards from behaviours, blending digital learning with authentic application, and treating assessment as a chance to practise the real decision — not recall a definition.

The Framework I Use

1
LearnFocused foundational knowledge — only what's needed on the job.
2
ExperienceRealistic scenarios using real tools. Makes the thinking visible.
3
AssessApplication-based decisions — a chance to practise the actual call they'll make.

Beyond the Role

Moments that shaped how I lead.

Because learning leadership isn't only about design — it's about judgment, ownership, and people.

Listening Before Assuming

While leading a project with two associates, I noticed one had become quieter in our daily huddles. Rather than guessing, I spoke with her one-on-one. She shared that she felt I was being more lenient with the other associate — someone I'd worked with longer.

Instead of defending myself, I acknowledged why she might perceive it that way. The difference came from familiarity, not expectations. But it was a real signal — differences in communication style can create a perception of unfairness.

I became more deliberate about consistency — in how I communicated, gave feedback, and checked in with both team members.

Ownership Beyond the Brief

While designing training for a major software release, I noticed the numbers on a new screen didn't match what the database was actually saving. When I raised it, I was told I'd misunderstood — and asked to adjust the training to match the screen.

I tested the feature step-by-step, recorded my screen, and pulled database logs to show exactly where data was being dropped. I translated the issue into business terms — wrong stock numbers, false out-of-stock alerts, wasted inventory — and brought QA in to verify.

Engineering fixed the bug before launch, we shipped training on time, and the product lead began involving our team earlier in future releases.
